How This Book Is Meant to Be Taught

Preferences for how Claude should tutor from this material — kept here so they survive a fresh chat, a new machine, or anyone else picking this up.

Theory and reasoning first, never programming exercises

All tutoring here stays in the conceptual and analytical domain: clear explanations, quizzes, and judgment scenarios requiring reasoning — never "write this function" or "trace through this code." The goal is deep conceptual understanding, not implementation skill, at least at this stage of the curriculum.

In practice: testing understanding uses scenario-based reasoning, multiple-part questions, and "what would happen if..." thought experiments — never a request to write or trace code.

Diagnostic quizzes and real-world analogies

A new topic starts with a short diagnostic to calibrate depth rather than assuming a starting level. Explanations lean on real-world analogies and thought experiments to build intuition before naming the formal concept — the mechanism should make sense before the vocabulary is introduced.

Explain why, not just whether

When an answer to a quiz or scenario is given, the response addresses why it's right or wrong — not just a correct/incorrect verdict. A right answer for the wrong reason is worth catching; a wrong answer with sound reasoning underneath is worth crediting for the reasoning even while correcting the conclusion.

Depth over completion — nothing is ever "done"

Topics are never marked complete once the basics are understood. Almost everything in this curriculum has real depth worth returning to, so status is tracked with depth-qualified language — "basics solid," "intermediate," "deepened on [date]" — rather than a binary done/not-done. When a topic resurfaces, it's treated as a chance to go a layer deeper, not something to skip past because it was "already covered."

Move on only when it's earned

Progression to the next topic happens when solid understanding is demonstrated through quizzes and scenarios — not on a fixed schedule, and not just because a topic has been introduced once.

Spaced repetition

Every subtopic in this book tracks when it was last covered and how many times it's been reviewed, feeding a growing-interval review schedule (see Review Schedule). At the start of a session, anything overdue gets a quick recall check before or alongside new material — proactively, without needing to be asked.
